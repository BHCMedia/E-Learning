WHY IT MATTERS

Spotting who's at risk, early

Identifying older adults at risk is one of the most important things we can do to prevent abuse before it starts.

01
PERSONAL RISK FACTOR · 01 / 12
RISK FACTOR

Mental illness or substance abuse

Affecting either the older person, or the individual providing their care.

02
PERSONAL RISK FACTOR · 02 / 12

Cognitive & physical decline

Dementia, or physical and mental decline from chronic illness or stroke, raises risk.

03
PERSONAL RISK FACTOR · 03 / 12

Family history & stress

Transgenerational violence, ongoing family conflict, stress — even issues like gambling.

!
WARNING SIGN

Left unchecked, it escalates

Personal and family pressures create environments where the potential for abuse can grow if unaddressed.

04
RELATIONSHIP RISK FACTOR · 04 / 12

Dependence & lost respect

A lack of respect for older people, or a deep dependence between carer and older person, raises risk.

!
RED FLAG

Any significant power imbalance

Care professionals must remain vigilant about power imbalance in the caring relationship.

05
SOCIAL RISK FACTOR · 05 / 12

Isolation silences reporting

Older people are often more socially isolated — making them less likely to report issues.

06
ORGANIZATIONAL RISK FACTOR · 06 / 12

Staffing & policy gaps

Inadequate staffing, skill mix, gaps in knowledge, and unclear policies all raise the risk of incidents.

07
ORGANIZATIONAL RISK FACTOR · 07 / 12

When leadership looks away

A poor response to allegations, or weak management, can create a culture where abuse goes unnoticed.

08
ORGANIZATIONAL RISK FACTOR · 08 / 12

Unsupervised, unmentored staff

Poor supervision of inexperienced staff can lead to unintentional neglect or harmful practices.

09
ORGANIZATIONAL RISK FACTOR · 09 / 12

Support systems matter

Demanding clientele and high-stress environments need strong support to maintain the standard of care residents deserve.

# Dementia Care Course Plan

## Final chapter structure

The course will be produced as **nine learning chapters plus a conclusion**.

Each section should be rendered as a separate MP4 so it can be uploaded individually to the existing eLearning system.

## Chapter 1 — Introduction

### Scope

- module purpose
- target audience
- five learning outcomes

### Status

- script approved
- storyboard prepared

### Suggested duration

45–55 seconds

## Chapter 2 — What is Dementia?

### Scope

- dementia as an umbrella term
- overview of the six common types:
  - Alzheimer’s disease
  - vascular dementia
  - Lewy body dementia
  - frontotemporal dementia
  - mixed dementia
  - early-onset and late-onset dementia

### Status

- chapter structure approved
- full narration requires final drafting and approval
- previous filmed-footage version must not be reused

### Suggested duration

2–3 minutes

---

## Chapter 3 — Symptoms of Dementia

### Scope

#### Cognitive symptoms

- memory loss
- difficulty performing tasks
- communication difficulties
- problem-solving difficulties
- impaired coordination and motor function
- confusion and disorientation

#### Psychological symptoms

- anxiety
- paranoia
- agitation
- depression
- hallucinations
- personality changes
- inappropriate behaviour and reduced inhibitions

#### Behavioural symptoms

- agitation
- resistance
- aggression
- anxiety-related behaviours
- wandering

### Suggested duration

3–4 minutes

---

## Chapter 4 — How Dementia Presents: Behavioural

### Scope

- behaviour may result from dementia-related changes in the brain
- it may be difficult to identify the exact cause
- communication difficulties can make causes harder to understand
- behaviour may also respond to changes in:
  - environment
  - health
  - medication

### Suggested duration

1.5–2 minutes

---

## Chapter 5 — Managing Behavioural and Psychological Symptoms of Dementia

### Scope

- what BPSD means
- behaviour and emotion may be linked to the underlying dementia
- behaviour is not necessarily intentional
- behaviour should not be taken personally
- BPSD may be distressing for the person and others
- behaviour may communicate frustration, distress or unmet needs
- there is no one-size-fits-all response

### Suggested duration

2–3 minutes

---

## Chapter 6 — Approaching BPSD

### Scope

- calm environment
- routine and consistency
- patience
- empathy
- non-confrontational communication
- avoiding sudden physical contact
- adapting support to the individual person

### Suggested duration

1.5–2 minutes

---

## Chapter 7 — Managing Specific Behaviours

### Section A — Restlessness and agitation

Cover:

- possible causes
- brain changes
- environmental factors
- sensory overload or insufficient stimulation
- unmet needs
- reassurance
- addressing discomfort
- calming activities

### Section B — Aggression

Cover:

- fear
- confusion
- environmental overload
- altered judgement and impulses
- communication difficulties
- pain or discomfort
- simple instructions
- redirection
- calm acknowledgement of feelings
- non-confrontational responses

### Section C — Hallucinations and delusions

Cover:

- poor eyesight or hearing
- physical illness
- mental health conditions
- changes in environment, carers or routine
- feeling overwhelmed
- staying calm
- acknowledging feelings
- distraction
- environmental adjustment
- observation when the person is not distressed

### Suggested duration

5–7 minutes total

### Production note

This may be delivered as:

- one complete chapter with three sections, or
- three shorter videos labelled Chapter 7A, 7B and 7C

The content and visual design should remain consistent either way.

---

## Chapter 8 — The ABC Approach to Behaviour Management

### Scope

- antecedent
- behaviour
- consequences
- debrief and decide
- identifying triggers
- documenting specific behaviour
- recording staff responses
- noting escalation or de-escalation
- learning the person’s triggers and calming strategies

### Suggested duration

2–3 minutes

---

## Chapter 9 — Strategies for Communicating with Someone Who Has Dementia

### Scope

- simple and clear language
- glasses and hearing aids
- active and patient listening
- recognising emotion behind words
- calm and respectful tone
- slow and moderate pace
- reducing noise and environmental distractions
- meaningful activities
- sensory stimulation
- physical movement and exercise

### Suggested duration

3–4 minutes

---

## Chapter 10 — Conclusion

### Scope

- compassion
- patience
- tailored and person-centred support
- each person’s experience is unique
- understand individual triggers and needs
- recap the major course messages
- direct learners to the final knowledge check in the eLearning system

### Suggested duration

1–1.5 minutes

---

## Overall estimated video duration

Approximately **22–29 minutes**, depending on the final Chapter 2 detail and whether Chapter 7 is one video or three short videos.

## Production direction

- one consistent AI-generated voice across the full course
- no logos
- no avatar
- no visible presenter
- no filmed footage
- no stock footage
- synthetic motion graphics, diagrams, icons and consistent illustrations
- Coral-inspired layout and fonts
- navy, teal, white and light-grey palette
- separate MP4 for each chapter
- separate WebVTT captions when supported
- quizzes and completion tracking remain in the existing eLearning system
